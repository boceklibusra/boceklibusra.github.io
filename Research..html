Research Experience

Graduate Research Assistant / Doctoral Researcher

Old Dominion University | 2023–Present

  • Led the development of empirical frameworks integrating Did You Feel It? (DYFI) macroseismic intensity with instrumental ground-motion data to estimate peak ground acceleration (PGA) and assess whether community-reported observations reproduce instrumental path-attenuation behavior.
  • Designed and implemented a large-scale data integration workflow combining 1,456 earthquakes, more than 86,000 instrumental ground-motion records, over 560,000 DYFI reports, and approximately 14,826 paired DYFI–ground-motion observations.
  • Developed and systematically evaluated linear, exponential, quadratic, and bilinear intensity-to-ground-motion conversion models incorporating magnitude, rupture distance, and VS30 to account for source, path, and site effects.
  • Established a model-evaluation framework using adjusted R2, mean squared error (MSE), residual diagnostics, hypothesis testing, and comparisons with established ergodic ground-motion models.
  • Investigated the robustness of DYFI-derived ground-motion estimates using both ZIP-code-based and 1-km geocoded intensity datasets and evaluated their implications for conversion modeling and path attenuation.
  • Co-developed a nonstationary, subregion-dependent spatial correlation framework for earthquake ground motions using multi-event Pearson correlations, Fisher transformation, spatial gridding, and Bayesian regression.
  • Evaluated regional variability in spatial ground-motion dependence across 322 California subregions and quantified improvements relative to a statewide stationary correlation model.
  • Contributed to the translation of research methodology into reproducible computational tools, including an open-source R implementation and GIS-based spatial parameter products for PGA, PGV, and spectral acceleration across multiple oscillator periods.

Selected Research Projects

DYFI-Based Ground-Motion Conversion and Path Attenuation Modeling

  • Research objective: Led research evaluating whether Did You Feel It? (DYFI) macroseismic intensity data can provide reliable ground-motion estimates and reproduce path-attenuation trends observed in instrumental recordings.
  • Methods: Designed and implemented a workflow integrating instrumental ground-motion records with both ZIP-code-based and 1-km geocoded DYFI reports; developed MMI-to-PGA regression models incorporating magnitude, rupture distance, and VS30; and evaluated model performance and attenuation behavior using residual diagnostics and statistical hypothesis testing.
  • Key result/contribution: Developed an upgraded DYFI-to-PGA modeling framework and demonstrated consistency between ZIP-code-based and 1-km geocoded DYFI datasets, supporting the use of community-reported intensity data for ground-motion and attenuation studies in regions with sparse instrumental coverage.

Nonstationary Spatial Correlation Modeling of Earthquake Ground Motions

  • Research objective: Contributed to research characterizing regional variability in earthquake ground-motion spatial correlation and assessing the limitations of applying a single stationary correlation model across California.
  • Methods: Co-developed analyses using multi-event paired ground-motion observations, Pearson correlation, Fisher transformation, spatial subdivision, and statistical/Bayesian regression to quantify spatially varying correlation structures.
  • Key result/contribution: Helped develop and evaluate a nonstationary spatial-correlation framework that captures regional differences in ground-motion dependence and provides a more locally representative alternative to statewide stationary models.
